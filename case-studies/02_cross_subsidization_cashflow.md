# Case Study: Detecting Hidden Cross-Subsidization and Forecasting a Cash Shortfall
### (Anonymized - methodology preserved, figures proportionally adjusted)

## Context

A public healthcare institution operates under a national health insurance
contract (a single-payer model, similar to an NHS-style contract): funding
comes from fixed service-package reimbursements plus revenue from paid
services. The institution consists of two divisions under one
administration: an operational medical division (which generates all
revenue) and a research/administrative division that lost its separate
state budget funding in 2026.

## Problem

Starting mid-2026, the institution's cash reserve began a sustained decline,
moving into deficit territory. Management was concerned but attributed this
to a general drop in the medical division's operating efficiency. No
department-level cost tracking existed - the institution had never
separated expenses by division at the management-accounting level.

## Method

Rather than accepting the "medicine became less efficient" explanation at
face value, an independent reconciliation was performed - comparing **two
independent data sources** on payroll:

1. The amount actually earned by **medical staff**, calculated directly
   from primary payroll data for the medical division's employees.
2. The amount **charged off by accounting** against the institution's
   overall balance under the payroll line item, for the same period.

The gap between (2) and (1) represents non-medical staff payroll that is
being charged against what is effectively the medical division's revenue,
since the administrative division no longer has its own budget.

Additionally, the medical division's operating result was recalculated in
isolation from the institution's overall balance - by subtracting only its
own direct costs (medical staff payroll, medical supplies, outside lab
services) from its own revenue (insurance payments + paid services) - to
separate medical operating efficiency from external cost burden.

## Findings

- **The medical division is operationally profitable.** Once isolated from
  the rest of the institution, it shows a positive result for the reporting
  month, despite the institution-wide deficit.
- **The overall deficit is explained by two external factors**, not medical
  underperformance:
  1. Scheduled investment payments for required diagnostic equipment (a
     mandatory condition of the insurance contract for one service package).
  2. Hidden cross-subsidization of the administrative division - the gap
     between earned and charged-off payroll, accumulated over 7 months,
     reached a sum comparable to 1–2 months of the medical staff's total
     payroll.
- **Liquidity forecast:** at the current deficit trend, cash reserves will
  be exhausted 3–4 months before year-end, putting timely salary payments
  and year-end contractual obligations at risk.
- **Risk of losing annual funding:** part of the service packages are
  funded up to an annual cap - at the current trend, that cap is projected
  to be reached earlier than year-end (a precedent from the prior year).

## Recommendation

A two-phase model was proposed:

**Phase 1 (immediate):**
- Set a hard monthly cap on non-medical payroll charged against the shared
  balance.
- Lock in an untouchable reserve to cover mandatory equipment payments and
  year-end obligations.
- Apply internal cost-cutting measures in the administrative division,
  mirroring a successful precedent from a prior crisis period.

**Phase 2 (structural, effective the following fiscal year):**
- Move to a transparent cost-allocation formula - a fixed fee for shared
  services (accounting, HR) or an allocation proportional to time actually
  spent supporting the medical division - replacing the current opaque
  "charge whatever is available" approach.

## Status & Impact

The analysis was presented to medical-division leadership with full
quantitative support (isolated operating result, reconstructed hidden
cross-subsidization, liquidity horizon forecast). Discussion of the
proposed measures is ongoing at the institutional leadership level as of
this case study's writing.

## Skills demonstrated

- Financial variance analysis / data reconciliation across independent
  data sources
- Separating operating performance from non-operating cost burden (cost
  allocation)
- Cash runway forecasting based on current trend
- Risk assessment and formulating actionable recommendations with
  measurable thresholds
- Communicating quantitative findings to non-technical leadership
